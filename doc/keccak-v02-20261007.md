## 3. Encoding conformance on hardware

`vkeccak.vi` with v0.2 semantics is legal only at `e64`, LMUL=8, `vl` a
multiple of 32, `vd` a group-aligned register, `vstart` at a group boundary;
everything else is reserved. A spec change of this shape is only half tested by
known answers — the hardware must also *reject* what became illegal, which is
exactly why pre-v0.2 binaries take SIGILL here.

riscv-pqc ships `edge_probe` for this (20 encodings, one process per case, an
illegal instruction expected to end the process), but its runner
`run_edge_cases.sh` only knows Spike and QEMU verdicts.
`tools/vkeccak_encodings.sh` is the native equivalent: same case table, same
per-VLEN expectations, trap judged by SIGILL (shell status 132). At VLEN=256
that is 7 legal and 13 reserved encodings. It needs `edge_probe0` from
riscv-pqc beside it (one binary covers all 20 cases, the case number is
`argv[1]`); the rootfs build does not install either, so both are copied to the
board by hand, as `/root/edge_probe0` and `/root/vkeccak_encodings.sh`.

**20/20 behave as specified.** The seven legal shapes each returned
`RESULT ok … bad=0` — correct Keccak-f output with every other vector register
untouched — and all thirteen reserved ones died with SIGILL:

| case | encoding | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 0, 1, 2, 14 | `m8` `vl=32`/VLMAX, `vd` = v0, v8, v24, v16 | legal, correct |
| 7, 16 | `vstart >= vl`, and `vstart=32` at `vl=VLMAX` | legal, no-op |
| 8 | `vl=0` | legal, no-op |
| 5 | **`vl=16`, not a multiple of 32** | trapped |
| 19 | `vd=v8` `vl=16` (the VLEN=128 form) | trapped |
| 12, 13, 17, 18 | LMUL m1, m4, m2, mf2 — too narrow for a 2048-bit group | trapped |
| 4 | `vd=v4`, not group-aligned | trapped |
| 6, 15 | `vstart=16` and `vstart=1`, inside a group | trapped |
| 3 | SEW=32 | trapped |
| 9 | `imm5=2` | trapped |
| 10 | `vill` set | trapped |
| 11 | `vm=0` | trapped |

Case 5 is the direct evidence for the `vl=25` question: a `vl` that is not a
multiple of 32 traps, so the September binaries could not have run here.

## 4. Cost of v0.2

### ML-KEM and ML-DSA: slightly faster

Full 14-variant matrix, `openssl_zvk_bench --quick`, and both `shake_bench`
configurations were re-measured on this bitstream into
`build/paper-data-20261007/` (not tracked; `build/` is ignored), with
`tab-pqc.tex`, `tab-micro.tex` and `tab-openssl.tex` regenerated from them by
karutxt's `date_tables.py`. The figures below are from that set.

Cycles per operation, 2026-09-15 (`vl=25`, bitstream `c61da577`) against today:

| | Sep 15 | 2026-10-07 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-768 KeyGen, `rv64gcv_vkec` | 2,483,031 | 2,436,568 | −1.9% |
| ML-KEM-768 Encaps, `rv64gcv_vkec` | 3,143,209 | 3,093,180 | −1.6% |
| ML-KEM-768 Decaps, `rv64gcv_vkec` | 4,218,570 | 4,207,802 | −0.3% |
| ML-KEM-768 KeyGen instructions | 114,910 | 95,822 | −17% |
| ML-DSA-65 KeyGen, `rv64gcv_intr_vkec` | 5,095,813 | 4,511,923 | −11.5% |
| ML-DSA-65 Sign, `rv64gcv_intr_vkec` | 26,321,190 | 23,218,985 | −11.8% |
| ML-DSA-65 Verify, `rv64gcv_intr_vkec` | 5,870,070 | 5,296,750 | −9.8% |
| ML-DSA-65 Sign instructions | 1,327,026 | 842,106 | −36.5% |

Mean speedup against RV64GC, from `tab-pqc.tex`: ML-KEM 1.48x -> 1.49x,
ML-DSA 1.65x -> 1.69x. `KeccakF1600_StatePermute()` reports the same output
fingerprint `6ea5537803894da3` on both dates, so the permutation result is
unchanged across the semantics change.

The `f1600` permutation counts are identical on both dates (43.06/44.06 for
ML-KEM-768, 188.00/287.30 for ML-DSA-65), so the same Keccak work is being
done and the difference is entirely in the surrounding wrapper, which `b3b6abf`
made leaner. The extra `vsetvli` per block that v0.2 requires is more than paid
for by what the rewritten `keccak.c` no longer does.

Attribution caveat: three bitstreams landed between the two dates, so this is
not a software-only A/B. For ML-DSA the −37% instruction count makes the
software the obvious cause.

Intrinsics still buy 1.2–1.4x over autovectorisation here: ML-KEM-768 encaps
3.10 M → 2.18 M cycles (29 ms at 75 MHz), ML-DSA-65 sign 31.3 M → 23.1 M.

### The permutation itself: +6 cycles

`shake_bench`, resident state, N=256, best of 7:

| row | Sep 15 (`vl=25`) | 2026-10-07 (v0.2) | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vkeccak.vi` 24-round, VRF resident | 87.98 | **93.98** | **+6.00** |
| wrapper around one permutation | 395.70 | 417.50 | +21.80 |
| absorb 168 B (SHAKE128) | 306.64 | 315.62 | +8.98 |
| absorb 136 B (SHAKE256) | 274.94 | 276.12 | +1.18 |
| squeeze 168 B (SHAKE128) | 292.44 | 292.86 | +0.42 |
| squeeze 136 B (SHAKE256) | 260.15 | 263.59 | +3.44 |
| vl switch pair (32 ↔ rate), loop only | — | 17.00 | new row |

The bare-permutation row is a like-for-like comparison: the timed loop is
`vkeccak.vi` plus one `addi` and one `bnez` on both dates, with the `vsetvli`
outside it (`tools/shake_bench.c`, `perm_only()`). So **the element-group
implementation costs about 6 cycles more per 24-round permutation**, 6.8% on a
single 2048-bit group at VLEN=256 — presumably the group-loop setup. Worth
confirming against the RTL, since at VLEN=256 there is only ever one group and
that setup has nothing to amortise over.

The new `vl switch pair` row isolates what the restart rules cost software:
17.00 cycles per block for the two `vsetvli` instructions (rate → 32 → rate),
about 8.5 cycles each. That, plus the +6, accounts for the wrapper row's
+21.80.

The same split shows up independently in `tab-micro`, whose
Keccak-$p$[1600,24] figure for the V intr.+K column went from **500 to 519
cycles and 18 to 21 instructions**: the three added instructions are the `li`
and the two `vsetvli`, and the rest is the instruction itself. So of the ~19
cycles v0.2 adds per permutation in that wrapper, about 13 are software the
restart rules require and about 6 are hardware.
